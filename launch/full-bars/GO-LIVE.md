# Full Bars: go-live checklist

As of 13 October 2026 the site (`sites/full-bars/`) is a preview, published only as a claude.ai artifact. Search engines are blocked, every page has a "not trading yet" banner, and it can't take orders, messages or trade-ins. The live shop needs its own web host on `www.fullbars.co.uk` (section 2); the artifact stays a preview copy. Work down this page in order. Nothing here is done unless it says so.

## Launch order

1. Accountant: VAT registration and the second-hand margin scheme. Then choose Shopify or Stripe links. ([§1](#1-decide-first-your-call))
2. Decide WEEE take-back, courier cover, the buy-now cap and the prices. ([§1](#1-decide-first-your-call))
3. Set up the company, bank, ICO fee, domain, web host, inbox, CheckMEND, Royal Mail account and waste carrier. ([§2](#2-business-and-accounts))
4. Fill every [placeholder], including the log-retention `[30]` in `privacy.html`. ([§3](#3-fill-every-placeholder))
5. Buy stock, IMEI-check it, photograph it, weigh one packed parcel. ([§4](#4-pictures-and-stock-needs-real-data))
6. Shopify: prove the CSV on one hand-made test product, then import and set up the store. ([§5](#5-if-you-choose-shopify)) Static site instead: ([§6](#6-if-you-stay-on-the-static-site))
7. Set up the order confirmation email (`order-confirmation-email.md` / `.html`) and test it with a test order. ([§5](#5-if-you-choose-shopify) step 11, or [§6](#6-if-you-stay-on-the-static-site) step 7)
8. Launch day: flip the switches, deploy the switched site to your web host and check it there, then put one real order through. ([§7](#7-launch-day-switches))

The files in this folder are listed at the end.

## 1. Decide first (your call)

1. **Checkout: Shopify, or the static site with Stripe Payment Links (`pay.js`).** I'd pick Shopify. Every phone is a one-off. Shopify counts stock per variant, won't sell the same phone twice, and keeps stock and payment in one place. A Payment Link can limit itself too: make one link per unit you hold, with "Limit the number of payments" set to 1, and Stripe deactivates it once that payment has completed. Stripe's docs don't say what happens to a second checkout already open at that moment, so you check each payment against `stock.js` before dispatch. And the site doesn't know about Stripe: after every sale you still clear the link from `pay.js` and lower `stock.js` by hand, then deploy the site, or the page keeps a Buy now button that no longer works. And a Payment Link charges the price it was made with, so every price change on a phone with a link means making a new link (`set_prices.py` lists them), where Shopify takes the change in admin. `pay.js` has a slot for each of the 183 storage and condition prices. Don't run both at once: you'd double-sell. With Shopify, the static site can stay up as the information front: `go_live.py --checkout shopify --shop-url https://…` points every Order button at the phone's Shopify product and switches the email-order route off (section 5, step 12), and with `--shop-variants` each button opens Shopify on the exact storage, condition and colour chosen (section 5, step 13).
2. **VAT, for your accountant (I'm not deciding this):** do you register for VAT? Registration is compulsory once taxable turnover passes £90,000 in any rolling 12 months, or as soon as you expect it to pass £90,000 in the next 30 days alone. And does the **VAT margin scheme for second-hand goods** apply to these phones? The answer changes four things:
   - the CSV's `Charge tax` column (currently TRUE);
   - whether receipts may show VAT (`terms.html` already says margin-scheme receipts won't);
   - the trade-in formula in `quote.js`, which assumes the margin scheme;
   - the VAT line in every footer.
3. **WEEE take-back.** You sell electrical equipment, so you may have retailer take-back duties, and the site says nothing about them yet. The duty (regulation 42 of the WEEE Regulations 2013) is worded for distributors that supply *new* electrical equipment, and I couldn't find official guidance on whether refurbished phones (or the new cable in each box) count, so ask, or play safe. Either join the Distributor Take Back Scheme (allowed if you sell under £100,000 of electrical equipment a year or sell only online; the current fee covers you to 31 December 2026, so budget for 2027), or offer your own free like-for-like take-back. Then add the wording to `delivery-returns.html` and `faq.html`. If you'll import phones yourself rather than buy them in the UK, ask whether that makes you a "producer" as well.
4. **Courier.** The site promises Royal Mail Special Delivery "or an equivalent service" (insured, tracked and signed for). Special Delivery's standard cover is £750. 13 of the site's prices are above that, the highest being the iPhone 16 Pro Max 1TB Excellent at £969, so those parcels need the £1,000 or £2,500 cover band.
5. **Buy-now cap** (static route only). `BUY_NOW_MAX` in `site.js` is £450, which covers 97 of the 183 prices. Keep it or change it.
6. **Prices.** The site calls them "preview prices". Check them against the market before launch. Change them with `set_prices.py`, never by hand: one price can show up in dozens of places (cards, "from" lines, titles, structured data, collection lines; cutting the iPhone 13's lowest price touches 39 places in 15 files), and the tool rewrites them all. From the repo root:
   - See what it would change: `python3 launch/full-bars/set_prices.py --set "iPhone 13" 128GB Good 199 sites/full-bars --dry-run`
   - Do it: the same command without `--dry-run`. Repeat `--set` to change several prices at once (for example Very Good and Good together, so a better condition never costs less).
   - If it lists anything under NEEDS A HAND EDIT (a phone crossing £300, the "Lowest price" badge, a trade-in price in `quote.js`, a Payment Link in `pay.js` for a price you changed), it writes nothing. Make that edit first and run it again, or add `--allow-hand-edits` and make the edit straight after.
   - Then rebuild and check the CSV: `python3 launch/full-bars/build_shopify_csv.py && python3 launch/full-bars/validate_shopify_csv.py`.
   - **Static route, once `pay.js` has Payment Links:** a link keeps charging the price it was made with, whatever the site says (a Stripe price can't be changed). `set_prices.py --set` lists every price you change that has a link in `pay.js`, for example "pay.js has a Payment Link for iPhone 13 128GB Good; Stripe still charges £209 … not £199". The edit to make first: deactivate that link in Stripe and clear its entry in `pay.js`. Then run `set_prices.py` again, make a new link at the new price with the same settings (payment limit included), paste it into `pay.js`, deploy, and check its Buy now button opens Stripe at the new price (section 6, step 1). Never change a price by hand in `data-prices` on this route: only `--set` knows the old price, so only it can name the link to replace.
   - **Shopify route, once the products are imported:** `set_prices.py` and the CSV rebuild change the site and the file, not Shopify. In the same sitting, make the same change in Shopify admin (Products, select the phone, **Bulk edit**, Price column: every colour of that storage and condition). Never import the CSV again to change a price: it would overwrite the live products with what the file carries (status draft, not published to the Online Store, stock 0), and can give the variants new IDs. Then run the price check, which compares every Shopify variant with the site's prices and must end "Price check: passed": `python3 launch/full-bars/fetch_shopify_variants.py --shop-url https://shop.fullbars.co.uk --check-prices --site sites/full-bars`. It lists any that differ (phone, variant, site price, Shopify price) and exits 3. Then deploy the site (section 7).
7. **Trade-in prices** in `quote.js` are copied from `lib/pricing-config.ts` in the phones-business repo. Confirm them before you open trade-ins.

## 4. Pictures and stock (needs real data)

- **Photos.** The CSV has no images. `og/products/<handle>-1x1.png` (plus 4x3 and 16x9 versions) are ready-made illustrations you can upload. Each CSV description already carries the site's "pictures are illustrations" line, so keep that line for as long as you use them. Real photos are better for refurbished phones.
- **Stock.** The CSV imports every variant at 0, and `stock.js` (the site's stock file) is empty. What stops a sale of a phone you don't hold depends on the route:
  - **Shopify:** the CSV sets "don't continue selling when out of stock" (`deny`), so a variant can't be bought until you give it stock. For each phone you buy and pass, add 1 to the exact variant (storage, condition, colour). Shopify's stock is what stops a sale on this route; `stock.js` only tells the static pages what Shopify has, so they show "Not in stock — ask us" instead of an Order button that lands on a sold-out Shopify page. Don't edit it by hand here: write it from Shopify with `fetch_shopify_variants.py --write-stock` (section 5, step 13), then fetch again and deploy the site whenever stock changes in Shopify, straight after you add stock and at least daily. Left empty, every option shows as orderable and Shopify shows what's sold out.
  - **Static site:** `stock.js` is the only thing on the site that stops it. Edit that file only (section 6, step 2), and run `set_prices.py --check` after every edit and before every deploy. (In Stripe, each Payment Link is for one unit, with a payment limit of 1, so Stripe deactivates it once a payment has completed: section 6, step 1.) `go_live.py --checkout stripe` refuses to launch while any phone has no stock data (section 7), and it marks the pages `data-stock-required`, so on the live site they fail closed: a phone `stock.js` doesn't list, or lists wrongly, and every phone if `stock.js` is missing or has a typo that stops browsers reading it, shows as not in stock, never as orderable. A phone it lists with no units shows "None in stock right now" on its cards, and "Not in stock — ask us" on its page.
  - **"From" prices (both routes, and the preview).** Every card, collection line, home-page tile, guide list and comparison table shows a phone's lowest price for any storage and condition, held or not, and every phone is a one-off, so that exact phone often isn't in stock. Inviting people to buy at a price you have reason to think you can't supply, without saying so, is an unfair commercial practice ("bait advertising": Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024, Schedule 20, paragraph 5). So every one of those lists, and the price on every product page, carries the line "Every phone is a one-off, so not every storage, condition and colour is in stock at every price, including the “from” price." Terms section 2 and the FAQ ("Is every storage, condition and colour in stock?") say the same. The line doesn't say each phone's page shows what you hold: that's true only while `stock.js` is up to date (kept by hand on the static route; on the Shopify route a snapshot that's right as of the last fetch), and never in the preview. Keep the line wherever "from" prices appear, including on any page you add. The titles and meta descriptions keep their "From £…" because the line is on the page.
  - Keep each phone's IMEI and unit number in your own records, never on the product page. The site's SKU style is `IP14-128-MID-VG-0247`, which is the CSV's variant SKU plus a unit number.
- **Replaced parts.** The site promises "If a part has been replaced, we tell you before you pay" and "Phones with any replaced part are sold by email order only". Shopify takes payment at checkout, and themes show a "Buy it now" button by default, so:
  - Only add Shopify stock for a unit with **all original manufacturer parts**. Every CSV description says "Every phone sold through this listing has all original manufacturer parts", and `validate_shopify_csv.py` checks that it does.
  - A unit with any replaced part stays at 0 on Shopify and is sold by email order:
    1. The customer asks through the contact page (the "Question about a phone" topic). You tell them which part was replaced and whether it's genuine.
    2. If they want it, take payment with a **Shopify draft order** (Orders > Drafts > Create order): add a *custom item* (not the standard listing) whose title names the phone and the replaced part, for example "iPhone 13 128GB Good, Blue, replaced screen (non-genuine)", at the site's price, add the customer's email, and send it from the draft's Payments section as an invoice. They pay through Shopify's checkout, the draft becomes an order, and Shopify sends the order confirmation (section 5, step 11) as for any order. Then send the unit email as usual, naming the part. Don't mark the draft as paid before they pay: that breaks the invoice link.
    3. Never a Payment Link: `pay.js` must stay empty on the Shopify route (`go_live.py` refuses otherwise).
    4. On the site, keep the sentences that say "Phones with any replaced part are sold by email order only", because they're still true. `go_live.py --checkout shopify` doesn't list a sentence just for saying replaced-part phones are sold by email order (so `grading.html`'s "Phones with any replaced part are sold by email order only, so you always hear about it before paying." stays as it is), but it does list one that also mentions Buy now, which doesn't exist on Shopify:
       - The FAQ's "Are they genuine phones?" says "Phones with any replaced part are sold by email order only, never through a Buy now button." In your `--rewrites` file, reword it without the Buy now part, for example "Phones with any replaced part are sold by email order only: ask us about one through the contact page."
       - `grading.html`'s next sentence, "Phones with a “Buy now” button have all original manufacturer parts.", for example "Every phone listed in our online shop has all original manufacturer parts."
       - Wording you write in `--rewrites` is never listed as checkout wording (it's your decision), so you don't need to add your new sentence as a key of its own. Keep passing the same `--rewrites` file on later runs. The CSV's line says the same, so leave it.
  - If you want such a unit on Shopify anyway, make it a separate listing whose title and description name the replaced part (for example "iPhone 13 128GB Good, replaced screen (non-genuine)"), and take that line out of its description. Never add it as stock on the standard listing. That makes "sold by email order only" untrue, so then rewrite those site sentences and the second sentence of `PARTS_LINE` in `build_shopify_csv.py` (rebuild the CSV afterwards) to say replaced-part phones are only sold through a listing that names the part, or by email order.
  - Static site: the same rule is in `pay.js`'s header. No Payment Link for a phone with a replaced part.
- **Weights.** The CSV uses the maker's handset weight plus a 200 g packing estimate (341–440 g). Handset weights live in one place: `weight_g` in `facts.json`, which `check_site_facts.py` checks every page against and `build_shopify_csv.py` reads. Weigh one packed parcel and change `PACKING_G` in `build_shopify_csv.py` (the packing, not the handsets), then rebuild the CSV.
- **Battery %.** Each unit's figure goes in the confirmation email within 1 working day. The site promises this.

## 5. If you choose Shopify

1. **Settings.** UK store, GBP. Prices must **include** tax, or Shopify adds 20% on top of these VAT-inclusive prices. Ask the accountant how to set `Charge tax` (the CSV has TRUE). Under the margin scheme VAT is still due on your margin, and Shopify won't calculate it, so it has to be worked out outside Shopify. With `Charge tax` on, Shopify's reports show VAT on the full price; with it off, £0. Neither is the margin VAT, so don't file returns from Shopify's tax figures.
2. **Shipping.** One free rate, UK only, as the terms say.
3. **Prove the format on one product first.** I couldn't open Shopify's help pages from my sandbox, so the CSV's column names and values (`draft`, `deny`, `TRUE`, `g`) haven't been checked against Shopify itself.
   - Make one test product by hand: options Storage, Condition and Colour with two values each; status Draft; not on the Online Store; track quantity on; don't continue selling when out of stock; physical product; charge tax ticked; weight in g; category "Mobile & Smart Phones".
   - Export it as a plain CSV. Download the sample CSV from Products > Import too.
   - Run `python3 launch/full-bars/validate_shopify_csv.py --template <the sample> --export <your export>`. Every line must say PASS or NOTE (a NOTE means your export leaves that column out, so there was nothing to compare). If only "your store uses the current column names" fails, import `products-shopify-legacy-headers.csv` instead. If a value line fails (say your store writes `DENY` where the CSV has `deny`), don't import: the builder needs changing first.
   - Delete the test product.
4. **Import** the file step 5.3 picked. You should end up with 19 draft products, 873 variants and 0 stock. Check that the category reads "Mobile & Smart Phones". Don't change any URL handle: each one must match its page on the static site (`products/iphone-13.html` is handle `iphone-13`), because that's where the site's Order buttons send people. Add stock only for units with all original manufacturer parts (section 4).
5. **Collections.** Make them automated, by tag, so they match the site:
   - "iPhone 12 series" to "iPhone 16 series": one tag each
   - "Samsung Galaxy", "Google Pixel": one tag each
   - Pro iPhones: tags `Pro` and `iPhone`
   - Big screen: tag `Big screen`
   - USB-C iPhones: tags `USB-C` and `iPhone`
   - Under £300: tag `Under £300`, which the builder recalculates from the prices on every rebuild

   The validator checks that the tags rebuild all 11 site collections exactly.
6. **Policies and trader details.** Put the section 3 trader details in Store details. Paste terms, privacy, delivery and returns into Settings > Policies.
7. **Wording that changes with Shopify.** `go_live.py --checkout shopify` lists every sentence below under "Checkout wording that doesn't fit --checkout shopify" and refuses to switch until each one is rewritten in your `--rewrites` file (or kept as it is, by giving it its own text as the new wording, if it's still true):
   - `terms.html` section 3 (how the contract is made). It describes email orders with a payment link and Buy now links, and says "In both cases the contract is made when we email to confirm your order has been accepted". Rewrite it for Shopify checkout: payment at checkout is your offer, Shopify's order email confirms you've received it, and the contract is made when you email to accept the order with the exact phone's details (the second email in `order-confirmation-email.md`). If you'd rather the contract was made at checkout, that's your call, but then the terms, the FAQ and both emails must all say so.
   - `terms.html` section 4: "Payment will be by card through Stripe, our intended payment provider". Listed on both routes: "intended" is wrong at launch.
   - `terms.html` section 2: "On an email order we confirm the colour and grade before you pay; if you pay straight away online, we confirm them by email within one working day, and if we can't match your choice, we refund you in full." It's listed because it mentions email orders. On Shopify, for example: "We confirm the exact phone, its colour and grade by email within one working day of your order; if we can't match your choice, we refund you in full." (Each product page's colour note, "subject to stock; confirmed by email", is true on both routes, so it isn't listed.)
   - Keep `terms.html` section 3's second paragraph as it is: the steps to buy online, how to check and correct your choice, that the contract is in English only, and how the contract is kept ("We keep a record of your order, as our privacy policy explains, and you can email us for a copy of it", plus the confirmation email carrying the terms of the day). The Electronic Commerce Regulations 2002 (regulation 9) require that information before anyone orders through a web checkout (contracts made only by email are exempt). It names no checkout and doesn't say the order can't be seen online, so it's true on Shopify too and `go_live.py` doesn't list it. Shopify does show each order online, on an order status page (and in the customer's account if you turn accounts on), so `go_live.py --checkout shopify` lists any sentence that says customers can't see their order on the website. If you want to mention the order status page, add a sentence such as "You can also see your order on its order status page, through the link in our order email." Only do that once your order confirmation links to it: Shopify's default template does, but `order-confirmation-email.html` replaces that template and has no such link, so add it there first. If you rewrite section 3 anyway, keep an equivalent of each of those four points.
   - `faq.html` "How do I pay?" (the answer and its copy in the structured data): Stripe, email orders, payment links, Buy now. Also "Are they genuine phones?" ("never through a Buy now button"; section 4, replaced parts, has the wording to use).
   - Done on 13 October 2026, so not listed: the FAQ's photos answer, the battery answer (FAQ and home page) and the photos line on every product page now say "ask us before you order", which stays true when ordering is paying (Shopify, or Buy now on the static route).
   - `grading.html`: "Phones with a “Buy now” button have all original manufacturer parts." Say instead that phones in your online shop have all original manufacturer parts (section 4 has an example). It's a sentence of its own, with no bold inside it, so one `--rewrites` key replaces it whole; the sentence before it, about replaced parts being sold by email order, is still true and isn't listed.
   - `contact.html`: the page title "Contact Us & Order by Email" and the message box's "For orders: …" prompt.
   - `privacy.html` must name Shopify and its payment processor instead of Stripe.
   - The site promises no tracking, but Shopify sets its own cookies. Turn on Shopify's cookie banner for UK visitors, add no marketing pixels, and rewrite `cookies.html` (its Stripe sentences are listed).
   - The Buy now note on each product page (`#buyNote`) is never shown on the Shopify route, so it's listed separately and needs nothing.
8. **Honest selling (DMCC Act 2024).**
   - Keep the site's "Every phone is a one-off…" line beside every "from" price (section 4), and on Shopify don't show a "from" price on a collection without the same line.
   - No compare-at prices unless it's a real previous price.
   - No countdown or "selling fast" apps.
   - No review apps until you have real reviews.
   - Stock messages only if they're true.
9. **Theme.** Dark, to match the site.
10. **Test.** Place an order in test mode, then a real order and refund on your own card.
11. **Order confirmation email (the law requires it).** Shopify's standard order confirmation doesn't include the cancellation rights, the cancellation form or your guarantee and warranty, and the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013 require the contract to be confirmed on a durable medium (email counts) with that information, no later than delivery. Without the cancellation information, the 14-day cancellation period can stretch by up to 12 months.
    - Fill the [placeholders] in `order-confirmation-email.html` with the section 3 values and a link to the current terms (a dated PDF is best: see the end of `order-confirmation-email.md`).
    - Paste it into **Settings > Notifications > Order confirmation** (Edit code), replacing the whole template. Use Preview.
    - Within 1 working day of each order, send the email in `order-confirmation-email.md` with the exact phone filled in (battery %, parts, and the IMEI ending only if you choose to show it). That accepts the order.
    - **Decide the IMEI question first (your call). It covers both files.** `delivery-returns.html` promises the phone's identifier never appears in emails. The `.html` template doesn't mention the IMEI. If you want the last 4 digits in the unit email, change that sentence on `delivery-returns.html` first (the top of the `.md` suggests the wording); only then add the IMEI ending to the `.md` email, and to the `.html` template if you like (its header comment, item 4). ## 6. If you stay on the static site

1. **`pay.js`.** Add a Payment Link only for a phone you hold that meets the rules in the file's header: original parts only, and at or under the cap.
   - **Price.** Make each link at exactly the site's price for that storage and condition, VAT included, as its Buy now button shows it. A Stripe price's amount can't be changed after it's made, so the link charges that amount until you replace it. If you turn on "Collect tax automatically" in the link, the price must include tax (Stripe's "inclusive" tax behaviour), or Stripe adds VAT on top of the site's price. If your accountant puts these phones on the VAT margin scheme (section 1, item 2), leave Stripe's automatic tax off: Stripe would work out and show VAT on the full price, but under the margin scheme VAT is due on your margin and isn't shown on the receipt (`terms.html`).
   - **Settings.** Require customers to accept your terms of service (`terms.html`), keep the quantity fixed at 1 (don't tick "Let customers adjust quantity"), collect the UK shipping address and phone number, and add a "Colour" custom field. One link per unit: tick **Limit the number of payments** and enter 1. Stripe then deactivates the link by itself once that payment has completed. Its docs don't say what happens to a second checkout that was already open at that moment, so two buyers paying at once isn't ruled out: check each Stripe payment against `stock.js` before dispatch, and refund any payment for a phone already sold (as well as reviewing every first order, as the header says). You can also change the message Stripe shows on a deactivated link. Turn on Stripe's email receipts (Settings > Business > Customer emails: under Payments, turn on **Successful payments**), so every buyer gets Stripe's receipt by email once the payment succeeds. In each link, under **After the payment**, keep the **Confirmation page** and replace its default message with your own, for example "Thank you: payment received. We'll email your order confirmation, with your cancellation rights and warranty, within one working day, before we dispatch." That matches step 7: the receipt isn't the order confirmation the law requires. Then add the Stripe Radar rules the header lists.

It can't see what Stripe charges, and nothing in this folder can: Stripe's API refuses any request without your API key, and that key must never be in this repo or on the site. Opening each link is the check (section 7).
2. **`stock.js`: edit this file only.** It's the one place stock is kept on this route. For each phone, its handle (the page's name: `products/iphone-13.html` is `iphone-13`), then the units you hold for each storage and condition, for example `"iphone-13": {"128GB|Good": 2, "256GB|Very Good": 1}`. The file's header shows the whole format. Colour isn't tracked, so the key is always `storage|condition`, counting every colour together, and the customer's colour is confirmed by email. A key that's missing, or 0, means not in stock: the button reads "Not in stock — ask us". When the storage and condition ticked in a product page's HTML isn't held, the page opened from a card (no choice in its address) opens on the cheapest storage and condition you do hold. `{}` means you hold none of that phone, and its cards then say "None in stock right now" (so do the home page's range tiles when every phone in the range is at none). `go_live.py --checkout stripe` refuses to launch while a phone has no stock data (or has it only in its page's older `data-stock`, below), a count isn't a whole number of 0 or more, a key isn't one of that phone's storage and condition pairs (a colour in the key is refused too), or a handle isn't a product page. Write the file exactly as its header shows (`window.FULLBARS_STOCK = { ... };` with JSON inside): the check refuses anything else, even a few forms a browser would run (single quotes, a count written `0x2` or `01`, an `<!--` line, `let` or `const`, a second `window.FULLBARS_STOCK =`), so what passes is exactly what the pages read. Change the count the moment a phone sells or arrives. **After every edit, and again just before every deploy, run `python3 launch/full-bars/set_prices.py --check sites/full-bars`** (or your switched copy's folder; `go_live.py ... --checkout stripe --check-only` runs the same stock check) and deploy only when it ends `RESULT: PASS`. It reads `stock.js` the way a browser does, using `go_live.py`'s own check, so it fails on a typo that makes browsers ignore the whole file (a stray comma, a missing brace, `{,}` left behind when you delete a phone's last count, a stray character outside the comment), a handle that isn't a product page, a key that isn't one of that phone's pairs, a count that isn't a whole number of 0 or more, a phone left out, a phone whose stock is only in its page's `data-stock`, or a page that has lost its `data-stock-required` mark, and names the line. If you forget and a broken `stock.js` goes up, the live pages fail closed: `go_live.py --checkout stripe` marks every product picker, card grid and row of range tiles `data-stock-required`, and there `site.js` treats stock data it can't read as nothing held.
