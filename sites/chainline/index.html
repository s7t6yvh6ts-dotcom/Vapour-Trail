    E-bike rules

    What makes an e-bike legal

    These are the rules in England, Scotland and Wales. The law calls a legal e-bike an electrically assisted pedal cycle (EAPC). Every e-bike we sell meets them.

    Meet these four rules, and have a maker's plate on the bike showing its motor power and its battery voltage or top assisted speed, and you don't need a licence, insurance, tax or registration.

    • Pedalsthat move it

      It must have pedals that can propel the bike.

    • 250Wmaximum continuous rated power

      The motor can't be rated above 250 watts continuous.

    • 15.5 mphassistance cuts out (25 km/h)

      The motor stops helping at 15.5 mph. You can go faster on your own legs.

    • 14+rider age

      The rider must be 14 or over.

    Throttles

    Without pedalling, a throttle can only power the bike up to 3.7 mph (6 km/h), as walk or start assist. A throttle that goes faster needs the bike to be type-approved, or in law it's a moped: registration, tax, insurance, licence and helmet.

    What we won't sell or fit

    Derestriction kits, also sold as delimiters, that lift the 15.5 mph cut-out.

    Conversion kits above 250W.

    Batteries

    We only supply the manufacturer's own battery and charger for each bike.

    Every used e-bike battery gets a capacity test. The result is shown as battery health: 94% means it holds 94% of the charge it held when new.

    Questions

    Before you buy

    How do you check a refurbished bike?

    Every bike goes through a 30-point check in our workshop. We look at the frame and fork for cracks and alignment, then the headset, bottom bracket and hubs, wheels and spoke tension, tyres, brakes, gears, chain wear and every bolt. Then we road test it.

    Anything worn gets replaced. The ticket on each listing shows what we fitted.

    What does the warranty cover?

    Three months on parts and labour from the day you collect the bike or it's delivered. If something we fitted or adjusted fails with normal use, we put it right.

    It doesn't cover crash damage, punctures or normal wear on tyres and brake pads.

    The warranty is on top of your legal rights under the Consumer Rights Act 2015. A bike must be of satisfactory quality for its age and price, and if it's faulty you can reject it for a full refund within 30 days.

    How does delivery work?

    UK delivery is £39 per bike. We box it 90% built. You fit the pedals and turn the bars straight, and the tools you need are in the box.

    Or collect it from the workshop for free and we'll set the saddle height with you.

    E-bikes go by a specialist courier because of their batteries.

    Can I return a bike?

    Yes. If you ordered online, you have 14 days from the day after you get the bike to change your mind, whether we delivered it or you collected it. Tell us, then send or bring it back within 14 days of telling us.

    We refund the bike and the delivery charge within 14 days of getting it back, or of you showing us you've sent it. Sending it back is at your cost: we can collect it for £39. If it's been ridden more than a test ride needs, we may take something off the refund.

    Bikes bought in person at the workshop can't be returned just for a change of mind, but your rights for faulty goods still apply.

    Do you service bikes you didn't sell?

    Yes. The workshop services any make, wherever you bought it, including e-bikes. See the service menu for prices.
