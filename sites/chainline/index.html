E-bike rules

What makes an e-bike legal

These are the rules in England, Scotland and Wales; Northern Ireland uses the same limits. The law calls a legal e-bike an electrically assisted pedal cycle (EAPC). Every e-bike we sell meets the bike rules below.

If an e-bike meets these rules and has a plate showing its maker, its motor power, and either its battery voltage or its top assisted speed, the law treats it like an ordinary bicycle: no licence, insurance, tax or registration.

  • Pedalsthat move it

    It must have pedals that can propel the bike.

  • 250Wmaximum continuous rated power

    The motor can't be rated above 250 watts continuous.

  • 15.5 mphassistance cuts out (25 km/h)

    The motor stops helping at 15.5 mph. You can go faster on your own legs.

  • 14+rider age

    The rider must be 14 or over.

Throttles

Without pedalling, a throttle can only power the bike up to 3.7 mph (6 km/h), as walk or start assist. Every e-bike we sell has no throttle, or one that stops at that speed.

A faster "twist-and-go" throttle is only legal on a type-approved bike or, in Great Britain, one first sold before 2016. Any other e-bike with one is treated as a moped: registration, tax, insurance, a licence and a helmet. We don't sell any e-bike with a faster throttle, whichever kind it is.

What we won't sell or fit

Derestriction kits, also sold as delimiters, that lift the 15.5 mph cut-out.

Conversion kits above 250W.

E-bikes with a throttle that works above 3.7 mph (6 km/h) without pedalling.

E-bikes with an "off-road" or "unlimited" mode that lets the motor help above 15.5 mph.

Batteries

We only supply the manufacturer's own battery and charger for each bike.

Every used e-bike battery gets a capacity test. We show the result as battery health: 94% means our test measured 94% of the battery's rated capacity, the watt-hours on its label. Range also depends on the rider, the route, the weather and the assist level, so it isn't a range promise.

Questions

Before you buy

How do you check a refurbished bike?

Every bike goes through a 30-point check in our workshop. We look at the frame and fork for cracks and alignment, then the headset, bottom bracket and hubs, wheels and spoke tension, tyres, brakes, gears, chain wear and every bolt. Then we road test it.

Anything worn gets replaced. The ticket on each listing shows what we fitted. We check the frame number on BikeRegister and only buy bikes with proof of ownership and ID.

What does the warranty cover?

Three months on the whole bike, parts and labour, from the day you collect it or it's delivered. If something fails with normal use, we put it right.

It doesn't cover crash damage, punctures or normal wear on tyres and brake pads.

The warranty is on top of your legal rights under the Consumer Rights Act 2015. Warranty and your rights in full.

How does delivery work?

UK delivery is £39 per bike. We box it 90% built. You fit the pedals and turn the bars straight, and the tools you need are in the box.

Email or phone us with your ticket number and what's wrong; photos help. Bring the bike to the workshop if you can. Your legal rights come first

This warranty doesn't reduce your Consumer Rights Act 2015 rights. A used bike must still be of satisfactory quality (taking into account its age, condition and price), fit for purpose and as described. In short:

  • Within 30 days of getting it, you can reject a faulty bike for a full refund.
  • After 30 days, we get one chance to repair or replace it. If that doesn't fix it, you can reject it for a refund (which, after six months, may be reduced to reflect the use you've had) or keep it with a price reduction.
  • Within six months of getting it, a fault is assumed to have been there when you got it unless we can show otherwise. After six months, it's for you to show it was.
