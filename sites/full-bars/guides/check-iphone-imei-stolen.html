How to check an iPhone’s IMEI before you buy

By the Full Bars team · Updated 1 October 2026

Every iPhone has a unique 15-digit number called an IMEI. Checking it before you buy a second-hand phone is the simplest way to avoid paying for one that’s been reported lost or stolen, or that a network will block.

How to find an iPhone’s IMEI

  • Dial *#06# in the Phone app. The IMEI appears on screen straight away. Phones with eSIM show more than one number; the first IMEI is the one to check.
  • Settings > General > About, then scroll down to IMEI.
  • The SIM tray on many models has the IMEI printed in tiny text.
  • The original box, if the seller has it. Make sure it matches the phone, not just a box.

The IMEI on screen, in Settings and on the box should all match. If they don’t, walk away.

How to check it

When a phone is reported lost or stolen in the UK, the network can block its IMEI, and the number can be recorded on databases that police, insurers and phone buyers use. Two places to check:

  • CheckMEND (checkmend.com) is used across the UK second-hand trade. For a small fee it checks the IMEI against loss and theft registers, insurance claims and network blocklists, and gives you a certificate. We check every phone we buy and sell on CheckMEND.
  • Immobilise (immobilise.com) is the free UK property register that police forces search when they recover stolen goods. Owners register their phones there, and it points second-hand buyers to checks they can run before paying.

A clean result doesn’t guarantee a phone was never stolen (a theft might not have been reported yet), but a flagged result is a definite no.

Check for Activation Lock too

An IMEI check won’t tell you whether the phone is still linked to someone else’s Apple Account. If it is, Activation Lock means you won’t be able to set it up after it’s erased. There’s no legitimate way around this without the original owner.

Before handing over money:

  1. Go to Settings and look at the top of the screen. There should be no name or Apple Account signed in.
  2. Check Settings > General > Transfer or Reset iPhone isn’t asking for someone else’s password.
  3. Ideally, see the phone on its “Hello” setup screen and get as far as connecting to Wi-Fi. If it shows “iPhone Locked to Owner”, don’t buy it.

Our guide to removing Find My before selling explains how the original owner unlinks a phone properly.

Warning signs when buying privately

  • The price is far below what dealers charge for the same model and grade.
  • The seller won’t let you see the phone switched on, or won’t tell you the IMEI.
  • They want to meet somewhere odd, or in a hurry, or only accept cash or a bank transfer up front for a posted phone.
  • The phone is “locked to my old account but it’s fine”. It isn’t.
  • There’s no box, no receipt and no explanation of where the phone came from.

If you think you’ve bought a stolen phone

Stop using it, keep all your messages with the seller and any payment record, and report it to the police on 101 or online. If you paid by card, speak to your card provider. Buying from a business also gives you rights under the Consumer Rights Act 2015: a phone that turns out to be blocked isn’t of satisfactory quality.
