How to check your iPhone’s battery health

By the Full Bars team · Updated 1 October 2026

Battery health is the single best clue to how hard a used iPhone has been worked. It takes ten seconds to check, and knowing what the number means stops you overpaying for a phone that will need a new battery next month.

In this guide

  2. What the percentage means
  3. Charge cycles (iPhone 15 and later)
  4. Messages to watch for

Where to find battery health

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Tap Battery.
  3. Tap Battery Health (on some models and iOS versions it’s called Battery Health & Charging).

You’ll see a figure called Maximum Capacity. That’s the number that matters.

What the percentage means

Maximum Capacity compares how much charge the battery can hold now with how much it held when it was new. A phone at 90% holds roughly nine-tenths of its original charge, so a day that used to end at 30% will end at around 20%.

All lithium-ion batteries wear with use, so a lower number isn’t a fault. Apple designs iPhone batteries to keep around 80% of their original capacity after a set number of full charge cycles, and treats 80% as the point where a battery service is worth considering.

Maximum CapacityWhat it means in practice
95–100%Like new. Either a young battery or a recent replacement.
88–94%Light wear. Most people won’t notice any difference day to day.
85–87%Moderate wear. Still a full day for most people; heavy users may top up in the evening.
80–84%Noticeably shorter battery life. Budget for a battery service in the next year or so.
Below 80%Worn. iOS may suggest a service, and performance can be managed to avoid unexpected shutdowns.

That’s why every phone we sell has a battery health of at least 85%. If a phone we buy in is below that, we fit a new battery before it’s listed.

Charge cycles (iPhone 15 and later)

On iPhone 15 models and later, you can also see the battery’s cycle count, its manufacture date and its first-use date. Go to Settings > General > About and scroll down to the battery section.

One cycle is 100% of the battery’s capacity used, not necessarily in one go: using 50% one day and 50% the next counts as one cycle. Apple says iPhone 15 and later batteries are designed to keep 80% of their original capacity at 1,000 cycles, double the 500-cycle figure Apple gives for earlier models. A low cycle count alongside a high Maximum Capacity is a good sign.

Messages to watch for

  • “Your battery’s health is significantly degraded” or a Service label: the battery needs replacing.
  • Peak performance management has been applied: the phone has shut down unexpectedly at least once, and iOS is limiting peak speed to prevent it happening again. A new battery fixes this.
  • “Unable to verify this iPhone has a genuine Apple battery” or Unknown Part: the battery has been replaced with a non-Apple part, or by someone who didn’t complete the pairing process. The phone still works, but battery health may not be shown accurately. You can see more under Settings > General > About > Parts and Service History.

We tell you before you pay if any part in your phone has been replaced, and whether it’s a genuine Apple part. Nothing is hidden in Parts and Service History.

What to look for when buying a used iPhone

  • Ask for the exact figure, not “good battery”. A reputable seller will tell you, or promise a minimum.
  • Check it yourself when the phone arrives, before you set it up fully. If it’s below what you were promised, you’re entitled to reject it: the phone isn’t as described.
  • Be wary of 100% on an older phone with no explanation. It may be a recent battery replacement, which is fine, but you should be told.
  • Weigh price against battery. A cheaper phone at 81% may cost you a battery service soon after.

Keeping your battery healthy

  • Turn on Optimised Battery Charging (Settings > Battery > Battery Health or Charging). It learns your routine and waits to finish charging past 80% until you need the phone. On iPhone 15 and later you can also set an 80% charge limit.
  • Avoid heat. Heat ages batteries faster than anything else. Don’t leave your phone on a car dashboard or charge it under a pillow.
  • Use decent chargers. Any reputable USB-C power adapter is fine. Very cheap unbranded chargers are best avoided.
  • Don’t worry about the occasional full charge or overnight charging. iOS manages this for you.
