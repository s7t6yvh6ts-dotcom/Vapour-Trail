Refurbished phone grades explained

By the Full Bars team · Updated 1 October 2026

“Excellent”, “Very Good”, “Grade B”, “Pristine”: every seller uses different words for the condition of a refurbished iPhone, Galaxy or Pixel, and there’s no official standard. Here’s what the grades usually mean, what ours mean exactly, and which one to choose.

Refurbished vs used vs “like new”

  • Used (or “pre-owned”) normally means sold as it was, perhaps with a quick wipe. Nobody has necessarily tested it.
  • Refurbished should mean the phone has been inspected, tested, repaired where needed, data-wiped and cleaned, then sold with a warranty. Ask what the testing involves, because the word isn’t protected.
  • Manufacturer refurbished phones are refurbished by the brand itself and usually come with a new battery and outer shell. They cost more and are sold by the manufacturer.

Whatever a seller calls it, UK law still applies. Under the Consumer Rights Act 2015, a refurbished phone must be of satisfactory quality, fit for purpose and as described, taking into account that it’s not new and the price you paid.

What the grades mean

Most sellers use three or four cosmetic grades. Some call them A, B and C; we call them Excellent, Very Good, Good and Fair. This is our published standard, the same for iPhone, Galaxy and Pixel, and the same whether we’re buying a phone or selling one:

We use Fair mainly to value phones people sell to us; the phones in our shop are Excellent, Very Good or Good, all with at least 85% battery health and a 12-month warranty. If we’re not sure between two grades, a phone gets the lower one.

We never sell phones with cracked glass, a faulty camera, faulty Face ID or fingerprint sensor, dead pixels, screen burn-in or an account lock (Activation Lock, Factory Reset Protection or Reactivation Lock), in any grade.

Grades are about looks, not how well it works

This is the bit people often miss. A Good-grade phone from a decent seller should work exactly as well as an Excellent one: same tests, same warranty, and at least 85% battery health on every phone we sell (higher on the top grades). The grade only describes how much the outside shows its age.

If a seller’s cheapest grade comes with a shorter warranty, a lower battery promise or “may have faults”, that’s a different product, not just a different grade.

Which grade should you buy?

  • Excellent if it’s a gift, if you’re going to use it without a case, or if you’re likely to sell it on later. A cleaner phone resells for more.
  • Very Good for most people. The marks are minor and disappear the moment you put a case on.
  • Good if you want the most phone for the money, or it’s for a child or a work phone that will live in a rugged case anyway. You can often move up a model for the same price.

Tip: compare Good grade of a newer model against Excellent grade of an older one. A Good iPhone 15 often costs about the same as an Excellent iPhone 14, and gets you USB-C and a newer chip.

Questions to ask any seller

  1. What exactly does each grade allow on the screen?
  2. What’s the minimum battery health, and is it the same for every grade?
  3. Has any part been replaced? If so, is it a genuine part?
  4. Is the phone unlocked, IMEI-checked and free of Activation Lock?
  5. How long is the warranty, and how long do I have to return it?

If a seller can’t answer those clearly, buy somewhere else.
