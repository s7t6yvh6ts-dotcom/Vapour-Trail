Which used iPhone should you buy in 2026?

By the Full Bars team · Updated 13 October 2026

A two- or three-year-old iPhone costs far less than a new one and still has years of software updates ahead. The hard part is choosing between more than a dozen models that look similar on paper. Here’s a straight answer for most budgets in 2026.

In this guide
  1. The short answer
  2. Side-by-side comparison
  3. What actually matters
  4. Prefer Android?
  5. What to skip

The short answer

  • Best for most people: iPhone 15. USB-C, the Dynamic Island, a 48MP main camera and a chip that will stay quick for years, for far less than its launch price.
  • Best on a tight budget: iPhone 13. Still fast, still a great camera, and noticeably better battery life than the iPhone 12.
  • Best small phone: iPhone 13 mini. The last iPhone mini. Battery life is the trade-off.
  • Best big battery on a budget: iPhone 14 Plus or 15 Plus. Huge screen, all-day-and-then-some battery, without Pro prices.
  • Best camera for the money: iPhone 15 Pro. 3x telephoto, ProRAW, titanium, the Action button, and support for Apple Intelligence.
  • Prefer Android? Galaxy S23 or Pixel 8. The Galaxy for zoom and a smaller phone, the Pixel for battery life and the longest support. More on Android.
  • Best overall if budget allows: iPhone 16 Pro or 16 Pro Max. 5x telephoto, Camera Control, and on the Pro Max the longest battery life of any model we sell.

What about the iPhone 17 range, iPhone Air and newer models? They’re still recent, so very few have reached the second-hand market at prices that make sense yet. We’ll add them once we can offer them with the same checks, battery minimum and warranty as everything else. If you have one to sell, we’re happy to quote for it.

What actually matters

Software support

Apple has a strong record of supporting iPhones with major iOS updates for around six to seven years after launch, and with security fixes for longer. The newer the model, the longer it will be supported. Every iPhone in this guide runs the current iOS 27, released in September 2026. If you want a phone to last you four years or more, lean towards the iPhone 14 or later. We’re happy to tell you the current support status of any model before you buy.

USB-C vs Lightning

iPhone 15 and later use USB-C, the same charger as most laptops, tablets and Android phones. iPhone 14 and earlier use Lightning. It isn’t a deal-breaker, but if your household is all USB-C, it’s one less cable.

Apple Intelligence

Apple’s AI features need an iPhone 15 Pro, 15 Pro Max or any iPhone 16 model (or newer). If those features matter to you, that narrows the choice quickly.

Pro or not?

The Pro models add a telephoto lens, (from the 13 Pro) a smoother 120Hz ProMotion screen and (from the 14 Pro) an always-on display. If you take lots of photos of people, pets or events, the telephoto is the upgrade you’ll notice most. If you mainly message, browse and scroll, a standard model is plenty.

Storage

128GB suits most people who use iCloud Photos. Choose 256GB if you shoot a lot of video or keep music and films offline. Storage can’t be added later.

Prefer Android?

We also sell two Android flagships, checked and graded exactly like our iPhones:

  • Samsung Galaxy S23: a compact 6.1-inch 120Hz AMOLED phone with a true 3x optical telephoto and the Snapdragon 8 Gen 2 chip. Samsung promised Android updates up to Android 17 and security updates until around early 2028.
  • Google Pixel 8: Google’s own phone, with a bigger 4,575mAh battery, a superb main camera and seven years of updates from its October 2023 launch, to around 2030.

Both use USB-C, are IP68 rated and charge wirelessly. Choose the Galaxy for zoom and a smaller phone, the Pixel for battery life and the longest support.

What to skip

  • The cheapest grade of the oldest model if you want the phone to last years. Spend a little more on a newer one.
  • Any phone without a battery health figure. See how to check iPhone battery health.
  • Network-locked phones. They’re cheaper for a reason. See unlocked vs network-locked iPhones.
