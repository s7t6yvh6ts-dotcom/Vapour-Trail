Unlocked vs network-locked iPhones: what’s the difference?

By the Full Bars team · Updated 1 October 2026

An unlocked iPhone works with any UK network’s SIM or eSIM. A network-locked one only works with the network that sold it. Here’s how to tell which you have, and what to do if your phone is locked.

In this guide
  1. What the difference is
  2. How to check if an iPhone is unlocked
  3. How to unlock an iPhone in the UK
  4. Why unlocked matters when buying used
  5. Network lock is not Activation Lock

What the difference is

For years, phones bought on contract from UK networks were often locked so they’d only work on that network. In December 2021 Ofcom banned UK mobile companies from selling locked phones, so phones bought from a UK network since then should be unlocked. Plenty of older phones, and some bought abroad, are still locked.

A locked phone works perfectly on its own network. Put in a SIM from another network and you’ll see a message such as “SIM not supported” or “SIM not valid”.

How to check if an iPhone is unlocked

  1. Go to Settings > General > About.
  2. Scroll to Network Provider Lock (on older iOS versions, Carrier Lock).
  3. If it says No SIM restrictions, the phone is unlocked. Anything else means it’s locked.

The most reliable test is simply to try a SIM from a different network. If you can make a call and use mobile data, it’s unlocked.

How to unlock an iPhone in the UK

Only the network the phone is locked to can unlock it properly. Contact them with the phone’s IMEI (dial *#06#). Most UK networks no longer charge to unlock phones, though some still require any contract or device plan to be paid off first.

Once the network has processed the request, put a new SIM in (or restart the phone) and follow any on-screen steps. Avoid paid “unlocking services” and software: at best they waste money, and at worst they relate to phones that aren’t legitimately the seller’s.

Why unlocked matters when buying used

  • Freedom to switch. SIM-only deals are often far cheaper than contracts, and an unlocked phone works with all of them, including virtual networks that use the big networks’ masts.
  • Travel. You can use a local SIM or travel eSIM abroad instead of paying roaming charges.
  • Resale value. Unlocked phones are easier to sell and worth more.

Every phone we sell is unlocked and tested with a SIM before it ships.

Network lock is not Activation Lock

These get mixed up a lot. Network lock is about which SIMs the phone accepts, and the network can remove it. Activation Lock is Apple’s anti-theft feature tied to the previous owner’s Apple Account. Only that owner can remove it, by turning off Find My. A phone with Activation Lock on is useless to anyone else. See how to remove Find My before selling and how to check an IMEI.
