How to turn off Find My and erase an iPhone before selling it

By the Full Bars team · Updated 1 October 2026

Before you sell, trade in or give away an iPhone, you need to turn off Find My and erase it properly. Skip this and the next owner can’t use the phone, your sale stalls, and your data could still be on it. It takes about ten minutes.

1. Back up

Go to Settings > [your name] > iCloud > iCloud Backup and tap Back Up Now. If you’re moving to a new iPhone, you can use Quick Start to transfer directly instead. Check WhatsApp and other apps with their own backup settings too.

2. Unpair your Apple Watch

If you have an Apple Watch paired to the phone, open the Watch app, tap All Watches, tap the info button next to your watch and choose Unpair Apple Watch. This also backs up the watch.

3. Turn off Find My

  1. Open Settings and tap your name at the top.
  2. Tap Find My, then Find My iPhone.
  3. Switch Find My iPhone off and enter your Apple Account password when asked.

Turning off Find My removes Activation Lock, the security feature that stops anyone else setting up your phone. Signing out of your Apple Account in the next step does the same thing.

4. Erase the iPhone

  1. Go to Settings > General > Transfer or Reset iPhone.
  2. Tap Erase All Content and Settings.
  3. Enter your passcode and Apple Account password if asked. This signs you out of your Apple Account and removes the phone from Find My.
  4. Wait for the phone to restart on the “Hello” screen. Don’t set it up again.

Don’t just delete your photos and apps by hand. That leaves your Apple Account signed in and Activation Lock switched on. Only “Erase All Content and Settings” does the job properly.

5. Sort out your SIM or eSIM

Take out your physical SIM. If you use an eSIM, the erase screen will ask whether to keep or delete it. If you’re moving your number to a new phone, follow your network’s instructions for transferring the eSIM first. Check you’ve removed any case, screen protector you want to keep, and that the phone is on the “Hello” screen.

Already handed it over? Remove it remotely

If the phone has left your hands still linked to your account, sign in at icloud.com/find on any device, select the phone, choose Erase This Device and then Remove This Device. Once removed, the new owner can set it up. You can also check Settings > [your name] on another Apple device to see the list of devices signed in to your account.

If Stolen Device Protection asks you to wait

If Stolen Device Protection is turned on and you’re away from somewhere familiar like home or work, turning off Find My needs Face ID and a one-hour security delay, then Face ID again. This is deliberate: it stops a thief who has watched you type your passcode from taking over your phone. Either wait the hour, or do it when you’re back at home.

Selling a Samsung Galaxy or Google Pixel?

Android phones have the same kind of protection. Factory Reset Protection ties the phone to your Google account, and Samsung phones can also have Reactivation Lock tied to your Samsung account. Take the accounts off before you reset, or the next owner will be asked for your password:

  1. Back up your photos and data (Google One backup, or Samsung Cloud or Smart Switch on a Galaxy).
  2. Sign out of your Google account: on a Galaxy, Settings > Accounts and backup > Manage accounts; on a Pixel, Settings > Passwords, passkeys & accounts (the menu name varies slightly by Android version). Tap the account and choose to remove it from the phone.
  3. On a Galaxy, sign out of your Samsung account in the same place. This also turns off Find My Mobile and Reactivation Lock.
  4. Factory reset: on a Galaxy, Settings > General management > Reset > Factory data reset; on a Pixel, Settings > System > Reset options > Erase all data (factory reset).
  5. Take out your SIM and any memory card, and check the phone starts at the setup screen.

Already sent it? Sign the phone out of your Google account at myaccount.google.com (Security > Your devices), and for a Galaxy, out of your Samsung account online too.
